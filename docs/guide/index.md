# What is TLCMap?

TLCMap — the Time Layered Cultural Map — is a tool for finding, publishing and
analysing Australian places in history and culture.

It does two things. It is a **gazetteer**: a searchable collection of millions of
Australian place records that you can query, map and download. And it is a **publishing
platform**: you can upload your own research data, map it, share it, and make it
searchable alongside everything else.

## What is in TLCMap

Everything in TLCMap is a *place record* — a name, a point on the map, and optionally a
date, a description and any other information the contributor supplied. Records come from
three sources, and you can search any combination of them:

| Source | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| **ANPS Gazetteer** | The Australian National Placenames Survey gazetteer — official Australian placenames, including historical ones. |
| **NCG Gazetteer** | The Composite Gazetteer of Australia. |
| **Layers** | Data contributed by TLCMap users and made public. |

A **layer** is a set of place records published together with its own title,
description, licence and other metadata. Layers are the unit in which people contribute
data, and the unit in which other people cite and reuse it.

## What you can do

**Find places.** Search by name, filter by place type, state, local government area,
date range or an area you draw on the map, then view the results as points, clusters or a
list. See [Searching for places](./search).

**Add your own data.** Upload a CSV, KML or GeoJSON file, or create records one at a
time — right-clicking a point on the map is the quickest way in. Your layer can stay
private while you work on it and be published when it is ready. See
[Preparing your data](./prepare-data) and
[Creating a layer](./create-layer#adding-a-place-straight-from-the-map).

**Map text.** Give TLCMap a document and it will find the placenames in it, locate them,
and turn them into a layer you can map. See [Working with texts](./texts).

**Share and cite.** Layers have persistent URLs and structured metadata, can be
downloaded as KML, CSV, GeoJSON or RO-Crate, and can be read by other software through the
Web Services API. See [Sharing your work](./sharing) and
[Exporting and feeds](./exporting).

**Analyse.** Run spatial and temporal statistics over a layer — how spread out the places
are, where the centre is, how they cluster in space or time, and how close one layer sits
to another. See [Analysing a layer](./analysis).

**Visualise.** Send any search result or layer to TLCMap Views to see it in 3D, as a
journey, as a timeline or as a flight over the terrain. See
[Visualising your data](./visualising).

## Do you need an account?

No — searching, viewing public layers, downloading results and using the Web Services API
all work without logging in.

You need an account to contribute data, save searches, or collaborate with other people
on a layer. Accounts are free. See [Accounts](./accounts).

## Where to start

- You are writing software against TLCMap: see the
  [developer documentation](/developers/).

::: warning A note on the data
Layers are contributed from many sources by many people, or derived by computer, and are
the responsibility of the contributor. Layers may be incomplete, and locations and dates
may be imprecise. Check the layer for details about its source. Absence from TLCMap does
not indicate absence in reality. TLCMap may inform heritage research but is not a
substitute for established formal and legal processes and consultation.
:::
